# Voiceover scripts (ElevenLabs)

Paste each block as one generation. `<break time="…" />` tags work on Eleven Multilingual v2 / Turbo / Flash.
On Eleven v3, replace them with "…" or `[pause]`.

Suggested settings: calm, warm narrator voice · Stability ~50 · Similarity ~75 · Style 0–15 · Speed 0.95.

Letters are written as capitals (A, N, M, X) so they are read as letters, not as the word "a".
If a letter still sounds wrong, spell it phonetically: "ay", "en", "em", "ex".

---

## Video 1 — What an exponent means (~125 words, ~50–55 s)

```
These two look almost identical… <break time="0.4s" /> but they're not equal.

First, what does an exponent actually mean?

Take five, and multiply it by itself. <break time="0.3s" /> One, two, three. <break time="0.3s" /> That's three factors of five.

We write that as five to the third.

The base is the number being multiplied. <break time="0.3s" /> The exponent counts how many times.

For example, one half to the fifth <break time="0.2s" /> is five halves multiplied together: <break time="0.3s" /> one over thirty-two.

Now, back to our puzzle. <break time="0.3s" /> With parentheses, the base is negative three.

Four negative threes multiply to positive eighty-one.

Without parentheses, the exponent only grabs the three. <break time="0.3s" /> The minus sign waits outside, <break time="0.2s" /> so the answer is negative eighty-one.

Parentheses change the base, <break time="0.2s" /> and that changes the answer.

So remember: <break time="0.3s" /> A to the N means N factors of A.
```

## Video 2 — The product rule (~100 words, ~40–45 s)

```
Five to the fourth, times five squared. <break time="0.3s" /> Can you simplify this without a calculator?

There's a shortcut. <break time="0.2s" /> Let's find it.

Write out every factor: <break time="0.2s" /> four fives, <break time="0.2s" /> times two fives.

Now count them all. <break time="0.3s" /> Six factors of five.

Four plus two makes six, <break time="0.2s" /> so the answer is five to the sixth.

Same base? <break time="0.2s" /> Just add the exponents.

And it works for any base: <break time="0.2s" /> A to the M, times A to the N, <break time="0.2s" /> is A to the M plus N.

Your turn. <break time="0.3s" /> X to the fourth, times X to the seventh. <break time="1.2s" />

Add the exponents: <break time="0.2s" /> X to the eleventh.

But careful: <break time="0.2s" /> the bases have to match.

Multiplying powers? <break time="0.2s" /> Add the exponents.
```

## Video 3 — Zero and negative exponents (~125 words, ~50–55 s)

```
Why is two to the zero equal to one, <break time="0.2s" /> and not zero?

The answer hides in the product rule.

Let it work with a zero exponent: <break time="0.3s" /> two to the zero, times two cubed, <break time="0.2s" /> is two to the zero plus three, <break time="0.2s" /> which is just two cubed.

So multiplying by two to the zero leaves eight unchanged, <break time="0.3s" /> which means two to the zero has to be one.

Any nonzero number to the zero power is one.

Now, a negative exponent. <break time="0.3s" /> Five to the fourth, times five to the negative fourth, <break time="0.2s" /> is five to the zero, <break time="0.2s" /> which is one.

So five to the negative fourth must be one over five to the fourth.

Check the pattern: <break time="0.2s" /> each step right divides by two. <break time="0.3s" /> Four, <break time="0.2s" /> two, <break time="0.2s" /> one, <break time="0.2s" /> one half, <break time="0.2s" /> one quarter.

Example: <break time="0.2s" /> negative two, in parentheses, to the negative third. <break time="0.3s" /> Flip it, then cube negative two. <break time="0.3s" /> The answer is negative one eighth.

Zero gives one. <break time="0.2s" /> A negative exponent means one over.
```
